How I got here

I started out drawing and designing real buildings. It was all I ever wanted.

I left the firm I trained at and could not land a client for months. My old Acer laptop, with 4GB of memory, could not run real design software. So I drew in Microsoft Paint. The work was good, but clients lost faith when they heard about Paint.

I planned to earn from code and pay for my dream of architecture. Then it stopped feeling like a backup plan. Both jobs are about building things that work in the real world, under pressure, over time.

I saw new companies ship products with nothing solid underneath. I saw owners buried in manual work, putting out fires all day. Different problems, one cause: no system, no plan, no automation.

I met a former Google software architect and saw it clearly. Designing software is like designing buildings. You plan for how people move and what they need. As an architect I designed buildings. Now I do the same for a business, and the building is the way you work.

Why I do this

I watched too many owners burn out doing every small job themselves. Chasing leads. Tracking work by hand. Working late just to keep up.

I know what it is like to look for a way out with little money and weak tools. And I know there is one: the right systems, automation and design.

I won't just throw you a tool. I'll build a system that fits the way you work, clears out what slows you down, and gives you your time back.
